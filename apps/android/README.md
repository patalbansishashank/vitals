# Vitals for Android

The Capacitor app: the Vitals web app as an installable APK (`desi.creative.vitals`, minSdk 26, target 36) that talks
to the ring over Bluetooth and keeps the link alive with the screen off. The generated `android/` project's build
folders, `dist/` and the signing keys are git-ignored.

## Build

Needs JDK 21 (Capacitor 8) and the Android SDK (platform 36, build-tools 35 or 36).

```sh
export JAVA_HOME=/path/to/jdk-21 ANDROID_HOME=/path/to/android-sdk
pnpm --filter @vitals/android run build:debug   # web build, cap sync, app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk
pnpm --filter @vitals/android run build:apk     # same, release: app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-release-unsigned.apk
```

`versionName` is the root `package.json` version and `versionCode` is `major*10000 + minor*100 + patch`, so every
release installs over the one before.

**Signing.** The release workflow signs the unsigned APK afterwards (`scripts/release/sign-apk.sh`, secrets
`ANDROID_KEYSTORE_B64`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS`, `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`). For a signed local
build, point Gradle at a keystore file through the environment; nothing about the key is written in the project:

```sh
ANDROID_KEYSTORE_FILE=/path/to/release.jks ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD=… ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS=… ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD=… \
  ./gradlew assembleRelease   # in apps/android/android; gives app-release.apk
```

Without `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_FILE` the release build stays unsigned.

## How it is put together

- **The web app** is the root Vite build (`dist/`), copied in by `cap sync`. Its origin is `https://localhost`, which
  the Vitals Server must list in `allowedOrigins`.
- **The ring** is driven from the web layer (`@capacitor-community/bluetooth-le` under the Capacitor transport in
  `src/biometrics/ble/transports/`), so every platform decodes the ring with the same TypeScript drivers.
- **`VitalsShell`** (`android/app/src/main/java/desi/creative/vitals/`) is the only native code: the foreground
  service with the "Ring connected" notification, ring alerts, the Bluetooth and boot receivers, files shared into
  the app and files saved out of it. The web side is `src/platform/androidShell.ts`.
- **Brand:** the adaptive icon, notification icon and the animated splash come from `brand/res/` (generated by
  `scripts/brand/`; do not edit by hand).
- **No service worker.** `sw.js` is left out of the APK (the APK already holds every file, and a worker in the
  WebView kept serving the previous build after an update); `src/platform/androidBoot.ts` unregisters one left by an
  older build.
- **No bridge logging.** `loggingBehavior: 'none'`: Capacitor's debug logging would print every Bluetooth call and
  result, ring frames included.

## Keeping the ring connected with the screen off

**Decision: the ring link stays in the web layer; the foreground service keeps the process, and so the WebView and
the Bluetooth link, alive.** The service holds no Bluetooth code of its own. One protocol implementation: the ring
drivers are TypeScript (`packages/rings`) and decode identically on the website, the desktop app and here.

Measured on the owner's phone (Android 16, the real ring), screen off for 15 and 20 minutes:
- the process, the service and the ring's Bluetooth link held (no drop, signal reads answered); after the app's own
  disconnect it reconnected by the stored address in 0.8–1.8 s;
- the WebView's renderer stays at the service's priority (foreground service, not cached, not frozen; MainActivity
  sets its priority policy), but its JS stopped about 20 s after the screen went off, for two reasons:
  1. **Chromium stops the renderer of a hidden window.** Holding the CPU awake changed nothing: a native event sent
     to JS waited until the screen came on. `RingWebView` (Capacitor's WebView, swapped in through the bridge layout)
     tells Chromium its window is still visible while the service runs. Nothing is drawn with the display off. The
     page's own `document.visibilityState` follows the real window (native `screen` event, `androidScreen.ts`), so
     what pauses off screen (live heart rate, the sync and planner timers) still pauses.
  2. **The CPU sleeps, and the WebView's timers count only the time it is awake**, so the ring service's own sync timer
     never comes due. `RingTick`: while the service runs, an alarm that may fire in idle wakes the phone (every 30
     minutes, the ring service's sync interval), holds a partial wake lock and sends `ringTick` to JS;
     `androidRingLink` reads every ring this device keeps (`syncNow`), gives sync 30 s to upload, checks "Ring
     disconnected" by the wall clock and calls `ringTickDone`, which lets the phone sleep (3 minutes at the latest).
  With both, every tick reached JS at once with the screen off (60-s test ticks at 105, 193 and 254 s). End to end
  (ring service, sync on to a test person, a read every 5 minutes for the test): screen off 15 minutes without
  opening the app, the server received 9 ring sample uploads and 129 sync changes after the screen went off, and the
  website showed 32 ring readings taken after it (newest heart rate 2 minutes before the screen came back on).
- Once (20 minutes in) Android dropped the renderer altogether; WebView's default then kills the app. The app now
  handles that: the process and the service stay and the activity starts over with a fresh WebView.

The alarm is inexact (an exact one needs a permission the person grants in settings): Android may move a tick later,
and in deep idle (phone unplugged and still) it spaces them out; a 30-minute read is well inside that. No wake lock
outside a read: the ring's own Bluetooth notifications wake the CPU when they come.

What it cannot do either: Android does not let an app open its own screen from the background, so after a phone
restart, or when the app was swiped away, the link is gone until the app is opened. The boot receiver then posts a
quiet "Tap to connect your ring" notification when a ring is known and "Keep my ring connected" is on.

`src/platform/androidRingLink.ts` decides when the service runs (from the first connection, while a ring is this
device's to keep, including while it reconnects), its text ("Ring connected", "Reading your ring…", "Looking for
your ring…"), "Ring battery low" (15 % or less, once per charge) and "Ring disconnected" (after 10 minutes).
