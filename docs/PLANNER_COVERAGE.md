# Planner evidence coverage

Generated by the evidence-coverage audit (`pnpm audit:planner`; the fast checks also run in `pnpm test`).
The audit asks whether the planner can use every piece of evidence the model encodes: whether each lever can appear
in a plan, whether each mechanism it relies on actually moves the numbers, whether every limit you set changes what
the planner may do, and whether any rule that removes options costs a better safe plan. Evidence grades never decide
anything here: they are recorded with each mechanism and shown, never used to drop or weight one.

## 2. Does each mechanism move its metric?

- Moves as stated: 1227; moves, but less than the uncertainty band: 209; does not move: 7; moves the other way: 0; differs between people: 1; no switch in the planner to test it: 41.
- Switching the mechanism’s model part off removes at least part of the effect for every tested mechanism, except 218.

## 3. Does the planner read what the mechanisms touch?

For every goal metric the planner records the series its mechanisms touch, and its fast mode gives the same goal values as the Simulator on 50 random plans (largest relative difference 0.0e+0).

## 4. Does every limit and consent change what the planner may do?

64 fields of the limits form, the consents, the screening and the training profile were changed one at a time on four requests.

Fields that change nothing the planner reads: the screening’s highest eligible fasting tier; the screening’s effective fasting tier; the screening’s list of opted-in tiers; the weights you own; exercises you like; your measured repetitions and one-repetition maxima.

## 6. Explanations

Every lever contribution an option states is re-checked by switching the lever off and running the plan again; every fasting comparison (“a plan with a fast was considered and lost because …”) is re-checked by re-running both plans. Both run in `pnpm test`.

## Fasting: which goals a fast can serve

The planner offers fasting for goal types (fat loss, glucose control, triglycerides and blood pressure, transient markers, hunger and adherence) and for every goal a fasting mechanism moves the right way (for example lower LDL or ApoB, more endurance capacity, less stored glycogen). Whether a fast is kept is left to the search: it stays only where it helps the ranked goals. Goals where the planner offers fasting although no mechanism says a fast helps (kept, because offering costs nothing):

- Lower blood ketones (bhb): fasts raise blood ketones in the model, never lower them; offered because ketones are a transient marker and the other direction is served.
- Raise igf-1: fasts lower IGF-1 in the model, never raise it; offered because IGF-1 is a transient marker and the other direction is served.
- Lower hunger pressure: hunger rises during a fast in the model; offered because the goal type covers hunger and adherence, and a person may simply prefer eating this way.
- Raise plan adherence probability: every fast lowers modelled plan adherence; offered for the same reason as hunger.
- Lower keto-induction symptoms: the keto-induction symptom index does not respond to fasts in the model; offered with the comfort goals.

## Limits of this audit

- Mechanisms are tested one at a time on three people; interactions between levers are tested only through the real planner runs of sections 1 and 5.
- The limits trace stops at the plan structures and setting ranges; whether a limit binds in the final plan is reported by the planner itself (“what each limit costs”).
- Rules applied inside the plan decoder (protein floor with training, productive training volume, surplus size, no late eating) are registered but not removed one at a time.
- 33 planner runs in parallel parts; run times are kept out of this file (qa/results/.timing/, not committed) so a release leaves the tree clean.
